## Workshop prompts (optional)

Copy the prompts below as you follow along. Replace bracketed placeholders with your own context. Use approved, redacted, synthetic, aggregate, or public information only, and review every output before using it.

Keep confidential records out unless your organization has approved their use. Check original sources, dates, geography, and requirements. Keep communications as drafts until reviewed.

## Lesson 1 — Improve a nonprofit request

### 1. Donor update example

> Draft a donor update for a fictional food-access nonprofit. July facts: 1,240 boxes; 410 households; 32 volunteers/186 hours; two distributions moved indoors during a heat wave. Write 180–220 words. Thank donors, include one specific source-backed program fact and a soft call to action. Use only these facts; do not invent outcomes, quotes, or stories. Ask if a fact is missing.

### 2. Try it with your own nonprofit task

> Help me [nonprofit task]. My context is [approved or synthetic information]. Give me [useful output] for [audience] in [format or length]. Use only the facts I provide. Ask if an important detail is missing.

## Lesson 2 — Research a nonprofit decision

### 3. Volunteer recruitment research example

> Research practical, current options for recruiting 20 adult volunteers for a Saturday community program in [CITY] within 14 days. Prioritize low-cost channels. Return a three-channel comparison, setup steps, risks or unknowns, and a 100-word outreach message. Use original or authoritative sources where possible, cite them, and flag anything requiring local confirmation.

### 4. Try it with your own research question

> Help me research [nonprofit question] in [location]. Show up to three options with original sources. Separate confirmed facts, recommendations, and items that still need checking.

## Lesson 3 — Volunteer welcome workflow demo

This is a connected-app demo. Follow along with the facilitator. Use the fictional practice Sheet and keep all four moves in the same Work conversation, with Google Drive and Gmail connected. Review approval requests before saving drafts, installing the Skill, or scheduling a task. Do not send emails, book calls, promise placements, or change the Sheet.

### 5. Move 1 — Read the interest responses

> Read Responses and Welcome Guide in this Sheet:  [Harbor Bloom Demo Files](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1yBi39wdQyh-JDDZtCgUfzWQNodUaxJ6B/edit?usp=sharing&ouid=106554420653084632405&rtpof=true&sd=true). Summarize every response with Follow-up status New, including older responses. Show the response ID, name, interest, volunteer availability, intro-call availability, and one personal detail. Flag missing names, emails, response IDs, or unclear information. Skip other statuses. Don’t change the Sheet or create emails yet.

### 6. Move 2 — Save welcome emails as Gmail drafts

> Follow the current Sheet’s Welcome Guide to save one personalized Gmail draft per complete New response. Use the subject “Welcome to Harbor House volunteering [Response ID]”. Check Gmail by recipient and exact response ID: skip matching Sent messages; update matching drafts instead of duplicating them. Use the approved intro-call request, formatting, and sign-off. Flag missing information or conflicting matches. Don’t book calls, promise placements, change the Sheet, or send emails.

### 7. Move 3 — Save the tested method as a Skill

> Use Skill Creator to create and install “Weekly Volunteer Welcome” from this tested workflow. Save the Sheet link, both tabs, and all our welcome, duplicate-check, draft-only, and no-Sheet-edit rules. Read the current Sheet each run and include all New responses, regardless of age. Don’t hard-code people or dates. Summarize drafts created or updated, skipped responses, and issues for review.

### 8. Move 4 — Schedule the weekly preparation

This example uses Monday at 9 a.m. Central. Activate a schedule only when the workflow is tested and you intend it to run; check the first run and review drafts before sending.

> Every Monday at 9 a.m. Central, run “Weekly Volunteer Welcome” against the current Sheet, following all its rules. Save or update unsent drafts and summarize results for my review. If a matching weekly task exists, update it instead of creating another.

## Capstone — Build a repeatable workflow

### 9. Try a recurring task for your role

> Help me [recurring job] using [approved sources]. Give me [useful output] for [audience]. Use only the information provided. Flag gaps or conflicts. Before any send, publish, schedule, or record change, show me a review checklist and ask for approval.
