# A veteran's guide to using ChatGPT Work for a career transition

## A guide for veterans and transitioning service members on using ChatGPT to translate military experience, build a civilian résumé, prepare for interviews, and develop AI skills for the workplace.

![Laura  Keenan](https://cdn.gradual.com/images/https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/Headshot-53798635-8187-41c2-bd0a-15cee49c1e98-1784555485528.jpeg?fit=scale-down&width=60)

![A veteran's guide to using ChatGPT Work for a career transition](https://cdn.gradual.com/images/https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/ChatGPT-Academy-Cover-12--9715e34c-356c-40dc-b3a4-2e40b51e184f-1785248728797.jpeg?fit=scale-down&width=1200)

Transitioning out of the military can bring excitement, uncertainty, and an entirely new set of questions.

What kind of work do I want to do next? How do I explain what I did in uniform? Will employers understand the responsibilities I carried? How do I describe my experience in a way that makes sense outside the military?

If those questions feel familiar, start here:

You have worked in environments that demand responsibility, teamwork, adaptability, and trust. You may have led people, coordinated operations, managed resources, trained others, maintained complex systems, or made decisions when circumstances changed quickly.

Those are not just military skills.

They are professional skills.

The challenge is helping your next employer understand them.

ChatGPT can help you do exactly that. It can serve as a practical transition partner: helping you put your experience into civilian language, research career paths, build a résumé, practice interviews, and develop the AI skills you can carry into your next workplace.

And you do not have to start from scratch.

OpenAI offers  [100 chats for veterans](https://chatgpt.com/use-cases/veterans/), a collection of practical, ready-to-use conversations created by veterans at OpenAI for other veterans. The collection includes support for the stages before, during, and after transition.

Eligible transitioning service members, veterans, and military retirees can also explore an offer for  [one free year of ChatGPT Plus](https://chatgpt.com/veterans-claim).

## Start by conveying the value of your service

A hiring manager may not know your military occupational specialty, the meaning of your rank, or what a particular assignment required.

But they can understand leadership, project management, logistics, problem-solving, communications, risk assessment, and operational planning.

ChatGPT can help you make those connections visible.

**Try this prompt:**

> I am a transitioning military service member or veteran. My branch of service was [branch], my role was [role], and my actual responsibilities included [responsibilities].
>
> Help me explain the value of my experience to a civilian employer.
>
> Identify the leadership, operations, technical, project management, training, communications, or problem-solving skills supported by my actual experience.
>
> Translate military terminology into plain professional language without erasing the fact that I served.
>
> Do not invent responsibilities, accomplishments, certifications, education, security clearances, personnel counts, budgets, or performance results.
>
> Ask me questions if you need more information.

## Show ChatGPT Work a strong résumé and build your own

Sometimes the hardest part of writing a civilian résumé is knowing what good looks like.

A résumé for an operations manager looks different from a résumé for a cybersecurity analyst, human resources specialist, project manager, or supply chain professional.

Instead of starting with a blank page, find a résumé example for the kind of job you want. Choose a sample you have permission to use. Then provide ChatGPT WOrk with your own military experience and the job description you are targeting.

Ask it to use the sample as a guide to structure—not as a source of accomplishments.

**Try this prompt:**

> I am a military veteran transitioning into [target role or industry].
>
> I am providing three things:
>
> 1. A sample résumé that shows the format and professional style I like.
>
> 2. My actual military résumé, service history, or verified description of my experience.
>
> 3. A job description for the position I want.
>
> First, explain what makes the sample résumé effective.
>
> Next, identify where my military experience aligns with the job description. Translate relevant military terminology into language a civilian employer can understand.
>
> Then create an original résumé for me using the sample’s general structure while relying only on my actual experience.
>
> Highlight verified experience in leadership, mission planning, logistics, training, project coordination, technical work, operations, or team management where relevant.
>
> Do not copy another person’s accomplishments. Do not invent dates, degrees, certifications, budgets, awards, security clearances, team sizes, or results.
>
> If a stronger résumé bullet requires information I have not provided, ask me a question.
>
> Finish by identifying three ways I can improve the résumé using additional details from my real experience.

For example, a veteran who coordinated personnel and equipment may have experience relevant to logistics or operations. Someone who trained junior service members may have experience relevant to learning and development. A veteran who maintained communications systems may have experience relevant to information technology or technical support.

The right description depends on what you actually did.

The goal is not to borrow someone else’s career.

**It is to present your own experience with the clarity and confidence it deserves.**

## Explore careers that fit your strengths

Your next job does not have to match your military title.

Start with the work you enjoyed, the responsibilities you performed well, and the kind of environment you want next.

**Try this prompt:**

> I am a veteran with experience in [describe actual responsibilities].
>
> I enjoyed [types of work] and want my next career to include [priorities such as mission, teamwork, location, flexibility, technical work, or leadership].
>
> Suggest five career paths that may fit my verified experience. Include options in government, the private sector, and mission-driven organizations.
>
> For each career path, explain:
>
> * Why my military experience may be relevant.
>
> * Which transferable skills I should highlight.
>
> * What qualifications I would need to verify.
>
> * What gaps I may need to address.
>
> * Two questions I could ask a professional in that field.
>
> Do not assume I have credentials or experience that I have not provided.

The  [veterans resource collection](https://chatgpt.com/use-cases/veterans/) also includes conversations about exploring new industries, researching federal employment, evaluating training and certifications, considering apprenticeship opportunities, and identifying careers that reflect what you most enjoyed about military service.

Use these responses to guide your research—not to make decisions for you.

Confirm job requirements, certifications, hiring processes, benefits, and veteran-specific eligibility against current, authoritative sources.

## Practice interviews before the real conversation

You have probably already briefed leaders, explained a plan, trained a teammate, or worked through a difficult situation under pressure.

A civilian interview asks you to bring those same abilities into a different setting.

**Try this prompt:**

> Act as a hiring manager interviewing a transitioning military veteran for a [job title] position.
>
> Ask me one interview question at a time.
>
> After each answer, tell me whether I clearly explained the situation, my responsibilities, the actions I took, and the outcome.
>
> Help me replace military acronyms with clear civilian language.
>
> Preserve the importance of my military experience and do not invent achievements.
>
> If my answer needs more detail, ask me a follow-up question.

## Bring your military discipline into your next workplace

The same habits that helped you succeed in the military can also help you use AI responsibly at work.

You already understand the importance of clear instructions, reliable information, defined responsibilities, operational boundaries, and human accountability.

Those are strong foundations for using ChatGPT effectively.

In your next workplace, ChatGPT Work can help you draft documents, summarize approved information, organize projects, prepare meeting notes, and think through unfamiliar tasks.

Where your organization provides access to ChatGPT Work, you can apply those same principles to a clearly defined, recurring workflow.

Start small.

Choose one task. Identify the information you are authorized to use. Define what a useful result looks like. Review the output before sharing it or taking action.

**Try this prompt:**

> I am a veteran starting a new role as a [job title].
>
> Help me improve this recurring work task: [describe the task].
>
> Use only the approved information I provide.
>
> First, identify the objective, required inputs, steps, and expected output.
>
> Then create a first draft of [briefing, checklist, meeting summary, project plan, or status update].
>
> Clearly identify information that is missing or uncertain.
>
> Do not invent facts, access additional systems, send communications, or make decisions on my behalf.
>
> End with a checklist of what I should verify before sharing the result.

Think of it as a familiar operating principle:

**Understand the mission. Confirm the inputs. Define the outcome. Review the work.**

## Protect what should stay protected

Military experience teaches an important lesson about responsible AI use: not everything belongs in a digital tool.

Do not enter classified information, sensitive operational details, protected personal information, security-sensitive material, medical records, or confidential employer information into a system that is not approved for that information.

Remove sensitive details before uploading résumés, performance reports, service records, work samples, or job documents.

Follow your organization’s AI and information-handling policies.

Verify important facts against trusted sources.

Review every résumé, application, recommendation, and work product for accuracy.

AI can help you prepare.

**Your judgment remains in charge.**

## Your next mission starts with what you already bring

You carry your experience, discipline, leadership, adaptability, and commitment to a mission with you.

ChatGPT Work can help you translate those strengths, build a résumé based on your actual accomplishments, practice for interviews, explore new opportunities, and develop practical AI skills for your next workplace.
