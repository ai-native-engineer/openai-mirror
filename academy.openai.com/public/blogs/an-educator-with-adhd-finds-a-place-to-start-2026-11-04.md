## Arfah Farooq shows ChatGPT her cluttered desk and asks it to talk her through the job, one step at a time.

![An educator with ADHD finds a place to start](https://cdn.gradual.com/images/https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/Arfah-Farooq-headshot-web-dba94dec-d6c4-41b2-abf2-60f2442eccf7-1793816126642.jpeg?fit=scale-down&width=1200)

An untidy desk can leave AI educator  [Arfah Farooq](https://www.linkedin.com/in/arfahfarooq/) unsure where to begin. Farooq, a self-employed consultant raising two young children, has ADHD. Alongside client work, she keeps track of school clothes and meals. “Your brain is just like, I can’t hold it in my head,” she says.

When Farooq was winding down a nonprofit, she knew what she wanted to tell its advisers. Putting it in writing was harder. Farooq, a self-employed AI educator who has ADHD, learned at the end of university that she also had dysgraphia, a difficulty with writing.

“You ramble a lot in your writing,” Farooq remembers hearing. Around 2020, a manager offered her help with dictation. She tried it, but after years of typing, she found it awkward to compose out loud. With an AI assistant, she could talk through an idea before she knew how she wanted it to read.

Farooq gave an AI assistant the nonprofit’s impact report and talked through what the organization had achieved in five years. She explained how she wanted to close it and asked the assistant to turn that account into a document she could share.

The ADHD shows up elsewhere in her life. When Farooq looks at a cluttered desk and can’t see where to start, she points her phone camera at it. “How do I sort this out?” she asks ChatGPT. It breaks the job into small steps. Sometimes she leaves the sound on while she clears the desk. “I’ve sorted that bit now,” she’ll say, and ChatGPT talks her through the next one. She calls it her version of “body doubling,” having someone else in the room to help her stay with a task.

When Farooq put new drawers in her wardrobe, she told ChatGPT what she actually wore. Most days she worked from home in comfortable clothes. When it was time to pick up her daughter, she wanted to reach in and grab a jumper and jeans. She asked how to arrange the wardrobe around that routine. Months later, she said, her husband could vouch for the result. “My wardrobe’s been clean for the last six months.”

ChatGPT even helps her manage caring for her children. Farooq’s four-year-old was starting school just as her six-month-old was starting solid food. Her daughter’s new teacher was coming to visit, and ChatGPT spotted the appointment on the calendar Farooq had connected. “Do you want me to come up with questions for the meeting?” she remembers it asking. Farooq said yes. ChatGPT drew on what she’d already told it about her daughter’s development, so that when the teacher arrived, Farooq had her questions ready.
