# Pets

## As their dog Oliver grew older, Troy Peterson and his wife, Catherine, used ChatGPT to make sense of his lab results and prepare questions for his vets.

![Caring for his aging Labrador, who walked the bride down the aisle](https://cdn.gradual.com/images/https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/troy-and-oliver-outside-169dccbb-6081-4ec4-934b-3d03529571e5-1793816357500.jpeg?fit=scale-down&width=1200)

Troy Peterson and his wife, Catherine, used ChatGPT to help their aging Labrador, Oliver, get better veterinary care as he faced arthritis and kidney disease. Catherine had adopted him after law school, and he was nine when she met Troy. He later walked her down the aisle in a little suit.

But Oliver developed arthritis around nine or ten, followed by chronic, serious kidney disease. His arthritis medications had to be adjusted repeatedly to reduce strain on his kidneys. The couple changed his diet and increased his bloodwork from every twelve months to every two or three.

They put Oliver's reports and lab results into a shared ChatGPT conversation, comparing new numbers with results from months earlier. It helped them understand treatment options and focus their worry and give them a game plan. They brought their vets a clearer history and researched options, then asked which course the vet recommended. Troy says those conversations helped the veterinary team give Oliver better care.

Nearly a year before Oliver died, sudden vertigo left him unable to stand. They rushed him to the emergency vet, fearing a stroke or tumor, and got an MRI. They used ChatGPT to understand the reports and discuss the next steps with their vets. Oliver survived the episode. Troy calls what followed “a full bonus year,” and credits AI with helping them gain that extra quality time and make it as full of joy as possible.

The decisions became harder as higher medication doses caused Oliver stomach trouble and accidents. Were they still giving Oliver a good life, or holding on for themselves? ChatGPT helped them work through that question with their vet and each other. Oliver still loved walks in the hills, enjoyed the sun, and sometimes chased a wild turkey with the energy of a younger dog. Those signs helped them see that treatment was still helping him.

Then Catherine called while Troy was out of town. Oliver had been acting strangely and began slipping in and out of consciousness as she got him to the vet. At the hospital, his temperature was extremely high, and he needed sedation. Around 5:30 the next morning, Troy called for an update. When the nurse said she would get the doctor, Troy knew they were losing him. The doctor said there was nothing more they could do.

Troy could not get home in time. On the phone, while Oliver could still hear his voice, he tried to say goodbye and choked on the words. He had a little time before the final farewell and turned to ChatGPT. It helped him explore his grief and find words: what Oliver had taught him, the memories they had made, and how much he loved him.

Catherine stayed beside Oliver with Troy on speakerphone. He got to say those words while Oliver's heart was still beating. Catherine had once hoped Oliver might live to 11 or 12. He was almost 14 and a half. Troy says being so well informed helped them feel they had done all they could for a companion who trusted them completely. “It was a tool that helped us love him well,” he said.
