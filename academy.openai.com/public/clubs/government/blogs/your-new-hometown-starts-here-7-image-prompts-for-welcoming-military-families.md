GOVERNMENT · VISUAL STORYTELLING · FICTIONAL EXAMPLES

The boxes are unpacked enough to find the coffee mugs. Now come the questions that make a new place feel like home: Where could we spend Saturday? Is there a library nearby? Where might we meet people?

For a military family arriving in a new community, a welcome campaign can start with those everyday moments. Installation communications teams and their local partners could use visual storytelling to introduce places to explore, programs to learn about, and opportunities to connect. The image opens the invitation; clear, accurate information helps a family take the next step.

This blog imagines a family discovering a fictional riverfront hometown. All images are AI-generated concepts. They do not depict a real installation, actual family, verified facility, or official military campaign. Each style offers a different way to tell the welcome story, with ideas that local, state, and federal agencies can adapt.

Think of this story as creative inspiration for using the prompts. The same styles can help explain a service, introduce a learning program, invite people to an event, celebrate local history, or explore an idea with colleagues. Change the subject, audience, and purpose to fit your situation. The examples are a starting point for your imagination, and your own context may suggest a very different story.

People engage with information in different ways. A layered scene can make several destinations easier to picture. A poster can spark curiosity. A collage can show the variety of everyday life. Pair the artwork with useful written information so the welcome reaches beyond the image.

## How to use these prompts

Use an image-enabled tool approved for your work. These visual prompting techniques are descriptive style cues to include in a prompt. Name the subject, medium, composition, and details to preserve. The examples share a fictional riverfront setting and color palette, but explore different visual treatments rather than exact character continuity.

## 1. paper cut illustration: Unpack a whole community

A moving box opens into a miniature neighborhood. Paper buildings, trees, and paths rise in layers, turning an ordinary moving-day object into a promise of discovery. This could introduce a welcome guide with a simple question: what will you explore first?

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-375390c0-bb18-4ebc-b4f4-3466e38380a7-1788974652956.png)

Fictional AI-generated illustration. A layered-paper neighborhood unfolds from an open moving box, with a small family at its edge.

### Try this prompt

> paper cut illustration
> Create a layered paper illustration of an entirely fictional riverfront community unfolding from an open moving box. Include a library, community center, homes, trees, and walking path, with a tiny civilian family discovering the scene. Visible cut edges, folds, rich layered shadows, low oblique camera angle. Teal, blue, green, cream, and small coral accents. No text, logos, actual installation layout, or map coordinates.

### Refine it

> Make the paper layers more distinct. Give each destination its own clear silhouette and simplify competing background detail.

### Adapt it for your agency

A city could introduce its public resources; a state could show the variety of places in a region; a federal agency could introduce public visitor experiences. Use this as an overview illustration. Link to a real directory or map for directions.

## 2. clay render: Make a place to gather feel approachable

Clay gives a community space a warm, handmade quality. Rounded furniture and small figures suggest a place for conversations and shared activities. An installation welcome team could use the concept to explore an illustration for a newcomer-event invitation or community-program introduction.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-c2949660-d2a0-44a4-a262-5cc676a0a6e0-1788974667330.png)

Fictional AI-generated illustration. A tactile clay miniature shows people gathering in an imagined community center.

### Try this prompt

> clay render
> Create a hand-sculpted clay miniature of an imagined community center with a broad open front. Show a welcome counter, seating, craft table, adults chatting, and a parent and child approaching together. Rounded forms, visible fingerprints, teal walls, cream floor, blue furniture, leafy plants, and soft directional light. Eye-level view at miniature scale. No text, logos, or actual facility claims.

### Refine it

> Keep the handmade texture. Reduce small props and make the welcoming interaction the focal point.

### Adapt it for your agency

A local library, state museum, or federal education team could use this style to introduce a fictional learning space. Before depicting an actual program, confirm the activities, participation requirements, and facilities shown. A playful illustration should not promise a service that is unavailable.

## 3. watercolor illustration: Picture an ordinary good afternoon

Not every welcome message needs to list services. Sometimes the story is a family enjoying a walk together. Soft washes and loose edges make this image feel like a moment someone might remember from a first weekend. It could accompany a local-partner feature about discovering the surrounding community.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-674a981f-f605-4f7a-aa0f-b15a81949c0e-1788974700402.png)

Fictional AI-generated illustration. A watercolor family walks toward a fictional riverfront town, with soft reflections and loose brushwork.

### Try this prompt

> watercolor illustration
> Paint an expressive watercolor of a fictional family, two adults and a school-age child in civilian clothes, walking along a riverfront path toward a small town. View them from behind, small within the landscape. Teal jacket, coral overshirt, blue backpack. Soft bridge and market awnings, shimmering water, warm afternoon light, pigment blooms, and visible cotton-paper texture. No text, uniforms, or identifiable real location.

### Refine it

> Preserve the loose brushwork. Leave more white paper around the edges and keep the family distinct from the landscape.

### Adapt it for your agency

A state tourism office could introduce an imagined weekend itinerary; a city could invite residents to explore local culture. Use verified photos or references when depicting a specific destination. Add actual access information, opening hours, and route details in accompanying text.

## 4. retro print: Turn discovery into an invitation

A travel-poster treatment gives the campaign a bold visual anchor. Limited inks, simplified landmarks, and textured printing suggest the pleasure of exploring somewhere new. Here, the headline connects the family’s arrival with the invitation to get to know the town.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-024a5f84-d6dd-40a6-9340-6c7c3ae928f6-1788974785223.png)

Fictional AI-generated illustration. A textured retro-style poster reads “YOUR NEW HOMETOWN” and “STARTS HERE” above an imagined riverfront scene.

### Try this prompt

> retro print
> Design an original retro-print travel poster for an invented hometown. Frame riverfront buildings and a pedestrian bridge with tall trees and a tiny family exploring. Bold simplified shapes, screenprint grain, slightly misregistered edges, limited teal, blue, green, and cream inks with a coral sun. Exact headline: YOUR NEW HOMETOWN. Smaller line: STARTS HERE. No other words, dates, real place names, seals, or logos.

### Refine it

> Increase headline contrast and keep the print texture away from letter interiors. Make the invitation readable at a glance.

### Adapt it for your agency

An installation and nearby town could develop a coordinated welcome concept; a state could use the treatment for regional discovery. Rebuild final lettering as editable text, check spelling, and add a clear next step outside the artwork, such as a link to verified newcomer information.

## 5. mixed media collage: Show the everyday pieces of belonging

A town is more than its skyline. Books, a garden, a walking path, and small shared activities tell a broader story. A collage gives these fragments a place together. It could help a team explore what a welcome campaign should include before selecting the final destinations and photographs.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-f4b26db8-101f-476b-a7d4-3d235faa12fd-1788974836233.png)

Fictional AI-generated illustration. A welcome scrapbook combines invented photographic fragments, illustrated leaves, paper shapes, and a moving-box cutout.

### Try this prompt

> mixed media collage
> Create a mixed-media welcome collage using entirely invented photographic fragments of a small-town riverfront, library books, adult hands planting in a community garden, and family shoes on a walking path. Combine torn paper edges, drawn leaves, teal brushstrokes, blue paper circles, cream paper, tactile tape, and a small moving-box cutout. Dynamic asymmetrical composition with a clear focal point. No text, actual maps, official documents, or logos.

### Refine it

> Keep four main fragments and give them more breathing room. Use a shared color treatment so the pieces feel related.

### Adapt it for your agency

A city could feature neighborhood life; a state could connect arts and recreation; a federal visitor program could preview different experiences. For a real campaign, use authorized photographs and captions identifying actual places. Label invented or proposed scenes clearly.

## 6. double exposure: Connect people with place

The family’s silhouette becomes a frame for the imagined community. The effect is symbolic: a place becoming part of someone’s story. It could work as an opening visual for a feature about settling in, paired with real, consented stories when a team moves from concept to publication.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-0885b587-c91b-477d-ab7d-5d8abd3e60c8-1788974859316.png)

Fictional AI-generated illustration. Family silhouettes blend with a fictional riverfront neighborhood, trees, and warm lights.

### Try this prompt

> double exposure
> Create an elegant double-exposure illustration of two adult parents and a school-age child as a family grouping. Blend their clear side-profile silhouettes with an imagined sunlit riverfront neighborhood, leafy trees, bridge, library windows, and warm lights. Keep the facial outlines recognizable while the landscape flows through them. Deep teal and blue, warm highlights, restrained cream background. All people fictional. No flags, uniforms, logos, or text.

### Refine it

> Simplify the interior landscape and strengthen the silhouette edges so the people and place remain easy to distinguish.

### Adapt it for your agency

A local agency could explore a community-belonging campaign, or a state cultural program could connect people with landscapes. Treat the blend as a metaphor. It does not represent an actual resident’s experience or testimonial unless the accompanying story is genuine and authorized.

## 7. surreal scale: Make a familiar object worth a second look

An enormous house key turns a familiar symbol into a surprising frame for the town beyond it. The small family provides scale, making the idea immediately playful. This could be the opening image in a welcome campaign, followed by practical information about discovering community resources.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-5ab245bb-9433-4342-9bd8-b1eeaa06c6ed-1788974878248.png)

Fictional AI-generated illustration. An oversized teal house key rests on a town green beside a path, framing an imagined neighborhood beyond it.

### Try this prompt

> surreal scale
> Create a photoreal surreal-scale scene: an enormous teal house key rests flat on a broad grassy town green. Its circular bow frames a distant fictional riverfront neighborhood and pedestrian bridge. A normal-size civilian family walks along an adjacent paved path, clear of the object. Low camera, strong depth, golden-hour light, realistic grass, metal, and shadows. Playful visual metaphor. No text, real installation, logos, or housing claims.

### Refine it

> Make the key unmistakable and the size contrast stronger. Keep the family visible and the town beyond the key in clear view.

### Adapt it for your agency

A city could use an oversized library card, a state could explore a giant picnic basket, or a federal museum could frame its story through an enormous magnifying glass. Choose an object connected to the message. Here, the key symbolizes discovery; it is not a promise of housing or eligibility.

## Try the style on a different story

Pick something your audience cares about, then borrow one visual technique from this blog. What could unfold in layers? What might feel more approachable in clay? What familiar object could become surprising at a different scale?

For example, a paper-cut scene could introduce the parts of a public service, a watercolor could illustrate a cultural-learning activity, and a collage could connect several perspectives in a community consultation. Start with your communication goal, try a few variations, and choose the image that makes your idea easier to understand. Creativity can travel far beyond this hometown example.

## Build a welcome campaign people can use

Start with one audience and one next step. For example: newly arriving families finding a community activity for their first weekend. Bring installation communications staff and relevant local partners together to choose the information the campaign should carry.

Use these styles to explore a direction, then choose the one or two that best serve the story. Seven examples demonstrate possibilities; a finished campaign benefits from a consistent visual approach. Reuse approved colors, recurring landmarks, and a reference image when continuity matters.

Keep essential information outside the generated artwork as editable text. Confirm dates, locations, costs, eligibility, registration, and access details. Give readers a short web address or contact option alongside a QR code. Avoid using a conceptual neighborhood as a real navigation map.

## Make room for different ways to arrive

This example shows a family with a child, but a broader welcome campaign can include service members arriving alone, couples, families of different sizes, and people with different access needs. Choose the stories with the audience rather than assuming one household represents everyone.

Provide descriptive alternative text, clear captions, readable contrast, and reviewed translations where useful. Ask newcomers whether the material helps them identify something they want to explore and find the information they need to do it. A welcoming image is a beginning; a usable next step makes the invitation worthwhile.

## A new place, a first connection

For military, local, state, and federal communicators, the opportunity is to make public resources easier to discover and connect them to ordinary life. A box, a book, a walk, or a key can open that story. Start with one experience your community wants to introduce—and show people how to learn more.
