# Your next adventure is public: 9 image prompts for parks outreach

## Turn a fictional parks campaign into nine visual storytelling examples for local, state, and federal government teams.

![Laura  Keenan](https://cdn.gradual.com/images/https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/Headshot-53798635-8187-41c2-bd0a-15cee49c1e98-1784555485528.jpeg?fit=scale-down&width=60)

![Your next adventure is public: 9 image prompts for parks outreach](https://cdn.gradual.com/images/https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/cover-green-blue-title-5bc22044-d821-45c8-9f38-edfca7db8216-1788967687234.jpeg?fit=scale-down&width=1200)

A picnic blanket on the grass. A trail disappearing into trees. A movie screen lighting up as the sun goes down. Parks offer moments people can picture themselves enjoying—if the invitation reaches them.

For government communicators, visual storytelling can help make that invitation clear. A city recreation team, a state park educator, and a federal public-lands office may use different channels, but each needs to help people understand what is available and how to take part.

People encounter information in different ways. Some will read a program guide. Others will notice a poster, watch a short video, or swipe through a few images. A coordinated campaign gives them several ways into the same story.

This guide imagines a fictional parks campaign, Your next adventure is public. The nine examples below show how one idea can become an ad, a video thumbnail, an event poster, and more. All images, landscapes, people, and event details are AI-generated concepts for practice.

## Start with the invitation

Use an image-enabled tool approved for your work. The techniques below describe visual styles and formats you can request in plain language. Paste the full prompt, then refine the result in conversation. To keep a campaign consistent, attach a reference image you are allowed to use and specify which colors, type treatment, and illustration details should stay the same.

Decide what you want someone to do: explore a park, watch a video, or plan a visit. Then choose the format. The examples use blue-green landscapes, tactile paper textures, and short headlines to connect the campaign without making every image look identical.

## 1. meta ads: Make the weekend feel possible

A city parks team is inviting residents to discover a nearby green space. A feed graphic can make an ordinary Saturday feel worth planning for. Give it one invitation and a scene people can picture themselves in.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-a05c3e76-d7ef-4f62-ad97-d0c07c446311-1788967870705.png)

AI-generated example. A winding trail connects a picnic, lake, and mountains beneath an invitation to find a park.

### Try this prompt

> meta ads
> Square promotional artwork for a fictional public parks campaign. Exact headline 'Your next adventure is public' and smaller 'Find your park'. Dramatic layered paper-cut landscape with a sweeping trail leading from foreground picnickers to a blue lake, teal forest and mountains; lively multigenerational visitors, warm sun, rich tactile shadows. Teal cyan sky-blue soft-green cream and navy. Large readable lettering with generous margins. No logos, seals, URLs or social platform interface.

### Refine it

> Make a vertical version, preserving the invitation and giving the headline more breathing room.

This is an artwork concept for Facebook or Instagram, not an ad campaign setup. Before use, adapt it to the actual placement and pair it with approved copy and a working destination.

## 2. thumbnail design: Show the surprise inside the video

A state park’s short video introduces things visitors might overlook. Let the thumbnail promise a specific discovery: a quiet overlook, a lakeside walk, or a new perspective. Pair the concept with real, verified footage before release.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-1b700e48-0630-4dd1-aadf-a8e90a985da5-1788967890484.png)

AI-generated example. A hiker looks toward a sunlit lake beside a short video headline.

### Try this prompt

> thumbnail design
> Wide 16:9 cinematic editorial video thumbnail for a fictional public park. Exact large headline 'A different kind of weekend' on left in cream bold type. On right, a joyful adult hiker seen three-quarter view looking toward a spectacular teal lake through towering trees; low morning sun and shafts of light, strong foreground framing, paper-cut contour-line accents, blue-green palette. One focal person, legible headline, no tiny text, no logos, no fake play button.

### Refine it

> Make the headline shorter: 'Find your outside'. Preserve the landscape and ensure readability at phone size.

## 3. typographic poster: Turn a few words into an invitation

A recreation center has one wall and a lot competing for attention. A type-led poster can create a moment of curiosity without explaining the entire program. Put practical details in nearby editable copy.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-100d8d02-a021-45e9-a539-4504dd1a8eae-1788967906518.png)

AI-generated example. Oversized lettering reads “Less scrolling. More strolling.” with trail and leaf details.

### Try this prompt

> typographic poster
> Portrait typographic park poster. Exact words 'Less scrolling.' then 'More strolling.' Extremely large expressive but legible dark navy sans-serif lettering. Letterforms interact with a curling paper-cut trail and small teal leaves without obscuring words. Cream textured paper, cyan blue soft green accents, sophisticated playful editorial design, generous margins. No other text or logos.

### Refine it

> Keep the words, remove half the decorative elements, and strengthen the contrast.

## 4. brand moodboard: Choose the world the campaign will live in

Before several offices make posters, videos, and signs, agree on a shared visual direction. A moodboard lets a county designer, state park educator, or federal public-lands communicator discuss what feels welcoming and distinctive.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-9a306f1c-9bd0-4038-a177-97ec11ebb451-1788967950076.png)

AI-generated example. A moodboard joins landscape imagery, color swatches, natural textures, and a headline sample.

### Try this prompt

> brand moodboard
> Landscape tactile editorial brand moodboard for fictional public parks campaign. Organised collage of teal cyan sky-blue soft-green cream and navy color swatches, topographic paper texture, leaf silhouettes, woven picnic blanket closeup, striking lake-and-forest photography, a paper-cut trail illustration and one typography sample saying exactly 'Find your outside'. Asymmetric but balanced grid, real material shadows, rich visual variety and generous spacing. No logos, seals, extra writing or color codes.

### Refine it

> Reduce this to a compact reference showing the palette, headline style, photography, and illustration treatment.

## 5. social carousel: Make each swipe open another door

A parks team can introduce different ways to enjoy the same place: walking, meeting friends, or lingering over lunch. The sequence should build an invitation rather than repeat the same headline four times.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-5efaa0f4-5ae0-4202-a3fc-4c485ce9c930-1788967969619.png)

AI-generated example. Four storyboard panels move from discovering the park to walking, picnicking, and spending the day.

### Try this prompt

> social carousel
> Wide contact sheet of exactly four equally sized square social carousel slides in one horizontal row with narrow cream gutters. Beautiful coordinated tactile paper-cut public parks campaign, teal cyan sky-blue soft-green navy cream. Slide 1 exact text 'Find your outside' with expansive lake and forest. Slide 2 'Take a walk' with winding trail and walkers. Slide 3 'Bring a picnic' with colorful picnic blanket and basket. Slide 4 'Make a day of it' with friends relaxing beneath trees. Bold readable headline on each, consistent typography, no additional words, no logos or URLs.

### Refine it

> Render slide 3 as a standalone square. Preserve its headline, palette, and illustration style.

This example is a storyboard, not a ready-to-upload carousel. Generate or export each slide separately in the required dimensions, then check the sequence on a phone.

## 6. event poster: Make the plan easy to find

An outdoor movie night gives a parks department a concrete invitation to share. Establish the reading order first: event, date, time, location, and cost. Every detail here is invented for the example.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-a8065f4b-17f9-4aac-a62e-73aaefb9d7c0-1788968023837.png)

AI-generated example. A fictional outdoor movie poster places the schedule above a twilight park scene.

### Try this prompt

> event poster
> Portrait event poster for fictional parks movie night, gorgeous cinematic paper-cut twilight park with people on picnic blankets looking toward a blank outdoor movie screen, teal trees under deep blue sky with soft green and cream highlights. Exact clear readable text: 'Cinema under the stars' largest; 'Friday, July 16'; 'Gates 7 p.m. · Film 8 p.m.'; 'Willow Lake Park'; 'Free admission'; small footer 'Fictional event example'. Generous text area and margins, strong hierarchy. No other text, real film imagery, logos, QR codes or real addresses.

### Refine it

> Increase the date, time, and location text. Keep the illustration below the essential event details.

Replace every invented detail with approved information, including the year. Add a verified page or registration link in the final layout, and keep the information available as ordinary text as well.

## 7. icon family: Help people scan the choices

A consistent set of icons can make a program guide easier to skim. Pair symbols with words: a picnic table may be obvious to one reader and unclear to another. Use verified standard symbols where formal signage requires them.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-64eb223d-a210-4216-9d29-e08e5abb6234-1788968080370.png)

AI-generated example. Six icons represent walking, picnicking, cycling, wildlife viewing, paddling, and outdoor cinema.

### Try this prompt

> icon family
> Exactly six coordinated park information icons in a spacious 3 by 2 grid on warm cream: walking boot, picnic table, bicycle, binoculars, canoe paddle, outdoor movie screen. Friendly clean geometric flat illustration, consistent navy outline thickness, teal cyan sky-blue and soft-green fills, equal visual weight, fully visible isolated icons. Crisp distinctive silhouettes, no text, numbers, logos, shadows or decorative extra icons.

### Refine it

> Show the set in one dark color and remove details that disappear at small sizes.

These are raster concepts, not a finished vector icon library. Refine and export individual symbols for production; use text labels alongside them.

## 8. seamless pattern: Connect the small details

A nature pattern can tie together a program booklet, tote, and event backdrop. Keep it subtle where people need to read. A generated tile is a starting point: inspect the repeated edges before sending it to production.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-75673cb7-c1da-49f1-a361-05c535fd3740-1788968128815.png)

AI-generated example. A proposed repeating tile combines leaves, trees, hills, flowers, and water ripples.

### Try this prompt

> seamless pattern
> Square seamless repeating park pattern tile, no border or margins. Airy rhythmic assortment of stylized teal leaves, cyan lake ripples, blue pine trees, soft-green hills, cream flowers and navy winding path marks on warm cream background. Sophisticated flat paper-cut-inspired design, motifs well spaced, consistent visual language. Match opposite edges precisely so repeated tiles connect seamlessly. No text, logos, gradients, mockups or shadows.

### Refine it

> Check a 2-by-2 repeat and correct broken motifs or visible seams. Preserve the airy spacing.

This image is a proposed tile. Use the repeat preview to inspect its joins; a seamless result is not guaranteed by the prompt alone.

## 9. sticker sheet: Give the visit a small souvenir

A visitor center can use a sticker concept to extend a campaign beyond the screen. Create a family of simple graphics that work individually. A printed sheet still needs separate artwork, sizing, and cut paths.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-269e650e-647c-4cb9-a245-552f7e558d25-1788968150702.png)

AI-generated example. Seven park-themed sticker concepts include a canoe, trees, hiking boot, picnic basket, binoculars, mountain, and slogan.

### Try this prompt

> sticker sheet
> Square sticker sheet with exactly seven separate die-cut-style stickers on pale blue backing. Cohesive beautifully crafted park campaign illustrations, teal cyan sky-blue soft-green navy cream; thick even white borders, ample space, no overlaps. Stickers: canoe on lake, pine-tree cluster, hiking boot with leaf, picnic basket, binoculars, moon above mountain, and typographic badge with exact words 'Find your outside'. Crisp charming silhouettes, flat front view. No other words, no logos, no QR codes.

### Refine it

> Create the canoe sticker alone on a transparent background with an even white border.

A white outline is not a printer cut path. Prepare individual files and confirm size, bleed, and cutting requirements with your production team.

## Make the next step as clear as the picture

A beautiful image earns attention. The next step should reward it. Connect the invitation to current hours, directions, fees, activity rules, and access information that your team has verified. For an actual park, use approved imagery of that place when visitors need an accurate picture of its trails or facilities.

Read every generated word, check small-screen legibility, and rebuild important text in an editable layout when practical. Include descriptive alt text and captions, and repeat essential details in the post or webpage so the image is never the only source.

Start small: choose one park program and make three connected pieces—a poster, a thumbnail, and a carousel. Show them to a colleague and ask, “What is this inviting you to do, and what would you need to know next?” Their answer will tell you what to improve.
