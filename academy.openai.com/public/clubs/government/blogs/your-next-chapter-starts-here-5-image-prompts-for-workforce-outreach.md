GOVERNMENT · VISUAL STORYTELLING · FICTIONAL EXAMPLES

Someone walks past a career center on the way home from a shift. They have been thinking about a change, but they are not sure what happens inside—or whether the place is for them. What could help them picture taking that first step?

For state workforce agencies and their local partners, that is a storytelling opportunity. A welcoming space, a conversation, or a glimpse of people learning can give an abstract service a human setting. Visual concepts can help a team explore how it wants an invitation to feel before developing a campaign or proposing a space.

This example imagines Next Chapter, a fictional state workforce center. We follow its story from a street entrance to a coaching room, a window display, a community fair, and an imagined district for learning. All five images are AI-generated concepts, not photographs of existing facilities, approved designs, or promises of services or employment.

Use this story as inspiration for the visual prompting techniques. Creativity can apply to many situations: a library introducing a learning space, a veterans program planning outreach, a federal visitor center welcoming the public, or a military team exploring a transition event. Change the audience, setting, and purpose to tell your own story.

## How to use these prompts

Use an image-enabled tool approved for your work. Include a visual prompting technique below as a descriptive style cue, followed by the subject, setting, composition, and details you want. These phrases describe the intended image. Copy an example, adapt it, and refine one detail at a time. For consistent architecture across several views, provide an approved reference image and specify what should stay the same.

## 1. architectural visualization: A doorway into possibility

The first image answers a simple question: what would make someone want to walk in? Warm light spills from a converted downtown building onto the pavement. People are visible inside. The center feels connected to everyday life on the street. A state agency could use this kind of concept to discuss the tone of an outreach campaign or gather reactions to a proposed location.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-70982644-f1ca-4969-b0e9-97a052fbaea4-1788976348204.png)

Fictional AI-generated concept. A fictional brick career center glows at dusk as visitors approach its glass entrance.

### Try this prompt

> architectural visualization
> Create a cinematic architectural concept of a fictional state workforce center in a converted brick downtown building at blue hour after rain. A glowing double-height glass entrance reveals people learning inside. A woman in a coral jacket approaches from the foreground, other adults enter, broad level approach, benches and street trees. Eye-level three-quarter view, wet pavement reflections, rich teal and warm amber contrast, credible human scale. Sign reads only NEXT CHAPTER. No real agency logos or seals. Inviting, active, editorial-quality wide landscape image.

### Refine it

> Keep the same building and camera angle. Make the entrance easier to identify, simplify the signage, and show a clear, level approach from the sidewalk.

### Adapt it for your agency

A city could explore reusing a vacant storefront, a state could introduce a proposed regional service hub, and a federal team could imagine a public-facing visitor entrance. Ask viewers what they think happens inside. Use their answers to improve the concept; an attractive entrance alone does not explain the service.

## 2. interior concept: Make the first conversation easier to picture

Now move inside. Instead of a room full of desks, imagine a place where someone can sit down and talk about what comes next. The image explores furniture, lighting, acoustic surfaces, and the relationship between open learning areas and quieter conversations. It gives staff and prospective visitors something concrete to react to.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-cd44472f-b343-4336-bff1-65ef637d78fb-1788976372332.png)

Fictional AI-generated concept. An imagined career coaching lounge combines round tables, semi-private alcoves, and shared learning space.

### Try this prompt

> interior concept
> Create a vivid architectural interior concept for a fictional state workforce center. View from behind an arriving visitor into a warm career coaching lounge: a career adviser and adult visitor talking at a round table at midground, several semi-private consultation alcoves, a shared digital learning table beyond, a quiet nook. Natural oak, teal upholstered seating, blue acoustic panels, soft daylight filtered by tall windows, plants, generous circulation, varied seating. Thoughtful lived-in details, realistic people and expressive depth, wide landscape. No text, no logos. Welcoming and adult, not corporate boardroom.

### Refine it

> Create a second version of this same room with a quieter consultation area and fewer visual distractions. Preserve the warm materials and show varied seating with clear circulation.

### Adapt it for your agency

A local adult-learning program could explore a study lounge; a state veterans team could imagine a mentoring room; a military transition team could discuss an orientation space. Ask whether the setting feels comfortable for the intended audience. Have facilities and accessibility specialists assess any design proposed for actual use.

## 3. retail window display: Turn a window into a question

What could your next chapter look like? Here, oversized open books become small worlds of learning. The display borrows the staging techniques of a shop window to introduce a public service. It could spark a conversation about new skills without asking someone to understand a full program brochure at a glance.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-c16456df-a956-4373-bd8b-8a3ff5a38e78-1788976405927.png)

Fictional AI-generated concept. A fictional career-center window displays oversized books and miniature learning scenes under the words “YOUR NEXT CHAPTER” and “STARTS HERE.”

### Try this prompt

> retail window display
> Visualize a striking fictional public career-center street window at dusk, using theatrical retail display craft for public outreach. Behind glass three oversized sculptural open books become miniature pathways into different careers: a solar-panel training model, healthcare learning props without patients, and a digital design workstation. Teal platforms, blue sculptural arches, warm focused lighting, cream backdrop. A passerby pauses outside, reflections remain subtle enough to clearly see the display. Large exact headline YOUR NEXT CHAPTER. Small line STARTS HERE. No other text or logos. Sophisticated tactile installation, dynamic diagonal composition, landscape.

### Refine it

> Keep the open-book idea, but feature only two learning pathways. Increase the headline contrast and make the display easy to understand from across the sidewalk.

### Adapt it for your agency

A library could stage a window around lifelong learning, a state museum could introduce a new exhibition, or a public recruitment team could showcase different kinds of work. Replace the fictional learning themes with verified offerings before publication. Pair the display with a simple, editable invitation explaining how to learn more.

## 4. exhibition booth: Bring the invitation to people

A center does not have to be the first point of contact. An outreach booth could bring the story to a community fair or regional event. This image explores an open layout, a recognizable overhead sign, places to ask questions, and room for a longer conversation. The people and interactions make the booth’s purpose visible.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-bcd39abc-4acf-4d82-bebe-5add938a3ce9-1788976444872.png)

Fictional AI-generated concept. An imagined teal-and-blue workforce booth offers an open welcome area and seated conversations at a community fair.

### Try this prompt

> exhibition booth
> Create a photoreal concept of a fictional state workforce outreach booth at a lively community career fair. Camera at a low three-quarter angle shows an open welcoming teal-and-blue pavilion with a luminous curved overhead banner reading NEXT CHAPTER. A staff member welcomes an adult visitor; another visitor using a wheelchair converses at a low table with clear knee space. Three simple visual stations with symbolic book, conversation, and compass graphics; comfortable side conversation area, open paths. Warm wood, fabric, hanging circular lights, convincing expo context with softly blurred neighboring booths. Dynamic human interactions, wide landscape. No QR codes, other text, actual logos or seals.

### Refine it

> Make the booth smaller and practical for a traveling team. Keep one welcome point, one seated conversation area, and an unobstructed entrance. Reduce decorative elements.

### Adapt it for your agency

A state workforce team could explore a mobile outreach presence; local agencies could coordinate a resource fair; federal or military teams could introduce career opportunities at an event. Use the image to discuss visitor flow, then confirm actual dimensions, staffing, privacy needs, and accessible features with the event team.

## 5. world building panorama: Show how the pieces could connect

The final image steps back. A career center, learning studio, workshop, and public courtyard become a connected fictional district. The panorama tells a bigger story: a first conversation might lead to exploring skills, meeting people, and learning something new. It is a way to discuss an ambition and the relationships between services.

![](https://d2xo500swnpgl1.cloudfront.net/uploads/oaiacademy/some-file-eb47f4fe-1b12-4ee4-813d-ae2463647c07-1788976491794.png)

Fictional AI-generated concept. A fictional learning district connects a career center, studios, workshops, and courtyards along a pedestrian route.

### Try this prompt

> world building panorama
> Build a sweeping imaginative but grounded fictional learning district for a state workforce storytelling campaign. Elevated wide-angle view at golden hour connects a converted brick career center, a glass digital learning studio, an open-door skilled trades workshop, a community college courtyard and a green public plaza along one walkable spine. Teal awnings and blue wayfinding accents unify the scene. Tiny people walk, learn, chat and arrive by bus; layered foreground trees and lively courtyard create depth. Warm light, detailed architectural concept art, optimistic human scale rather than science fiction. Wide landscape panorama. No text, real place, logos or official claims.

### Refine it

> Bring the camera lower and focus on three connected destinations. Make the route between them visually clear and keep the scene grounded in an ordinary town.

### Adapt it for your agency

A state could imagine how education and employment partners connect; a city could explore a civic learning district; a military installation could picture a cluster of transition resources. Label it as an imagined environment. It can support a vision discussion, but it is not a real map, funded development plan, or verified service pathway.

## Use the images to learn something

Give each concept a question. Does the entrance look welcoming? Can a visitor picture what happens in the coaching room? Is the window’s message clear? Could a booth support both a quick question and a longer conversation? Does the panorama explain a connection people care about?

Share early concepts with staff and people who might use the service. Ask what they notice, what they assume, and what remains unclear. Revise around those responses. If a picture suggests a service you cannot offer, change the picture or the accompanying explanation.

## Make the invitation usable

People engage with information in different ways. Pair images with plain-language descriptions, descriptive alternative text, readable type, and reviewed translations suited to the community. Put the next step in text people can copy or read aloud. Do not rely on the picture, color, or a QR code alone.

For a real campaign, verify locations, hours, offered services, costs, eligibility, and contact details. Represent actual access features accurately. Keep important wording editable and review generated signs before using them. When describing a real facility, use verified photographs or approved design references and make the status of any proposed changes clear.

## Borrow the technique, change the story

These five prompts can travel well beyond workforce outreach. Architectural visualization can introduce a proposed public space. An interior concept can explore how a room supports a conversation. A window display can invite curiosity. A booth can take information into the community. A panorama can connect several ideas in one scene.

Start with what you want someone to understand or do. Then choose the visual approach that helps them picture it. For our fictional career center, the story begins with a person passing a doorway and wondering about a change. Your agency’s next story may begin somewhere entirely different.
