# The Briefing Deck, Rebriefed

## What changes when ChatGPT joins you inside PowerPoint

Government teams do not need more slides. They need clearer stories, stronger evidence, and an easier path to the decision. ChatGPT for PowerPoint can help public servants turn approved reports, notes, spreadsheets, and existing decks into editable presentations—while keeping people firmly in charge of the facts, framing, and final briefing.

## It is 10:07 a.m. The briefing is at 2:00.

You have a 46-page report, two spreadsheets, three emails full of context, and last year’s presentation—still wearing a title slide from a different fiscal year.

The assignment sounds simple: “Can you turn this into a brief?”

Of course.

﻿ [ChatGPT for PowerPoint](https://chatgpt.com/apps/powerpoint/) changes what happens next.

It lives in a sidebar inside Microsoft PowerPoint, where it can help create, edit, understand, and polish presentations without turning the deck into a flattened, uneditable artifact.

The opportunity is not simply to make slides faster. It is to spend less time moving words into boxes and more time deciding what the room actually needs to understand.

That is a much better use of everyone’s afternoon.

## Begin with the ideas, not the slides

Before choosing a chart or debating whether a title should be 28 or 30 points, ask a more important question:

**What story does this briefing need to tell?**

Give ChatGPT the approved source material, audience, purpose, and decision. Then ask it to propose the presentation’s narrative before it creates anything.

> Review the approved report, spreadsheet, and previous presentation attached.
>
> I need to brief [audience] on [topic]. The purpose of the briefing is to [inform, align, recommend, or secure a decision].
>
> Before creating slides, propose a concise narrative with:
>
> * The bottom line
>
> * What changed
>
> * Why it matters
>
> * The evidence
>
> * Available options
>
> * Risks and unresolved questions
>
> * The decision or next step
>
> Use only the attached sources. Clearly identify any information that is missing or uncertain.

Now you have something worth reviewing: the spine of the briefing.

Move a section. Challenge an assumption. Remove the background everyone already knows. Add the question leadership will certainly ask.

Then build the slides.

## Make every title earn its place

Many government decks are full of perfectly respectable titles that say almost nothing.

“Program Status.”

“Budget Update.”

“Key Considerations.”

These are labels. A strong briefing title communicates the conclusion.

Try asking:

> Rewrite the slide titles as concise, evidence-based conclusions. A person reading only the titles should understand the presentation’s central story.
>
> Do not create a conclusion that is not supported by the slide. Flag slides where the evidence is insufficient or the takeaway is unclear.

“Program Status” might become:

**The program remains on schedule, with two dependencies requiring leadership attention.**

Suddenly the title is carrying its share of the briefing.

## Invite the difficult questions early

A briefing rarely fails because the font was wrong.

It fails because the argument was unclear, the evidence was thin, the recommendation appeared too quickly, or the room asked a question the deck never considered.

ChatGPT can provide a useful first challenge before the presentation enters formal review.

> Review this deck as a skeptical member of [audience].
>
> Identify:
>
> * The three claims you would challenge
>
> * Evidence that appears incomplete or unsupported
>
> * Assumptions that have not been stated
>
> * Options or tradeoffs that are missing
>
> * Questions you would ask during the briefing
>
> * The slide most likely to confuse the audience
>
> Do not rewrite the presentation. Give me a prioritized critique first.

This does not replace legal, policy, technical, communications, accessibility, security, or leadership review.

It helps the deck arrive at those reviews better prepared.

## Use the audience switch

The same core facts may need to work in several rooms.

A program team may need operating detail. A senior leader may need the decision and risk. A partner may need responsibilities and dependencies. A public audience may need plain language and additional context.

Instead of rebuilding the presentation from the beginning, ask ChatGPT to recommend what should change.

> Create a version of this presentation for [new audience].
>
> Preserve all approved facts, dates, requirements, and official language.
>
> For each slide, recommend whether it should be kept, simplified, expanded, removed, or sent for additional review. Then create the revised version.
>
> Do not change the meaning of policy, guidance, or official determinations.

One source of truth. Different levels of altitude.

Much less copy-and-paste confetti.

## Let the data make a point

A chart should answer a question—not merely confirm that a spreadsheet exists.

With an approved table or spreadsheet, you can ask ChatGPT to create an editable chart slide and write the takeaway as the title.

> Using the approved table attached, create one editable slide for [audience].
>
> Select the chart that most clearly communicates the central finding. Write an evidence-based conclusion as the slide title, include relevant limitations, and add a source note.
>
> Verify the calculations before creating the chart. Do not imply causation from correlation.

Then check the chart against the original data.

Every number. Every label. Every source.

The chart may be polished. The math still needs a human.

## Ask what the deck is not saying

One of the most useful questions you can ask ChatGPT for PowerPoint is also one of the simplest:

> What is missing?

You can make it more specific:

> Based on the stated purpose and audience, what information, evidence, perspective, risk, or decision is missing from this presentation?

Or more pointed:

> If this briefing ends without a decision, what is the most likely reason?

That question tends to bring the presentation back to its actual job.

## Keep the brief in briefing

ChatGPT can also help with the unglamorous but highly valuable work of making a deck shorter.

Ask it to identify repeated content, move background detail into an appendix, reduce dense paragraphs, and simplify slides without changing their meaning.

> Reduce this presentation to no more than [number] core slides for a [length]-minute briefing.
>
> Preserve the evidence, decision, risks, and required official language. Move useful supporting detail into an appendix rather than deleting it.
>
> Explain every recommended removal before changing the deck.

The objective is not minimalism for its own sake. It is giving the audience enough information to understand, question, and act.

## Your deck can stay editable. Your standards should stay high.

ChatGPT for PowerPoint can create a first draft from source material, revise existing slides, build charts, turn screenshots into editable content, and help examine the presentation’s story.

It can also make mistakes.

It may misread a source, alter text unexpectedly, create an unsupported takeaway, or produce slides that do not perfectly follow an organization’s template, fonts, or formatting requirements.

For government work:

* Use an approved account and workspace.

* Use only information authorized for the tool and environment.

* Duplicate important presentations before making substantial changes.

* Preserve required templates, footers, markings, disclaimers, and slide dimensions.

* Verify facts, numbers, quotations, calculations, and citations.

* Check every chart against its underlying data.

* Complete all required clearance and approval processes.

* Keep official determinations and consequential decisions with authorized people.

Editable does not mean approved.

Polished does not mean correct.

And a very attractive chart can still be wrong.

## Getting started

Open Microsoft PowerPoint, go to **Home**, select **Add-ins**, and search for **ChatGPT**. Add it, open ChatGPT from the PowerPoint ribbon, and sign in with the account authorized for your work.

Access may depend on your organization’s Microsoft 365 configuration, ChatGPT workspace settings, administrator controls, permissions, and rollout.

Once it is available, start with a real presentation and a contained assignment:

* Diagnose the story.

* Rewrite the titles.

* Find the missing question.

* Simplify three dense slides.

* Challenge the recommendation.

* Build one chart from approved data.

You do not need to hand over the whole deck on day one.

## The slide is not the product

The product is shared understanding.

It is the moment when the room sees the issue clearly, trusts the evidence, understands the tradeoffs, and knows what needs to happen next.

ChatGPT for PowerPoint can help public servants reach that moment with less friction and a stronger first draft.

The final story—and the responsibility for telling it—still belongs to you.
