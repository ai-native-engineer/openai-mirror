Legislative work moves quickly. A bill changes, a hearing approaches, and the Member needs a clear briefing. This prompt pack gives U.S. House offices structured starting points for turning approved source material into useful drafts.

Download the PDF below for 30 prompts across six legislative workflows.

## Who this is for

Designed for Legislative Directors in U.S. House Member offices, the pack also supports legislative assistants, Chiefs of Staff, and staff who prepare policy briefings or coordinate legislative work. Adapt each prompt to your office’s responsibilities, committee assignments, and Member-approved priorities.

## Why it is useful

Each prompt defines the task, the inputs, the expected output, and what to verify. That structure can help staff spend less time setting up a first draft and more time evaluating evidence, resolving questions, and preparing the Member. The prompts ask for sources, distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions, and flag missing information.

## What is inside

• Legislative priorities: weekly LD agendas, 90-day work plans, portfolio handoffs, research assignments, and accomplishments briefs.
• Bill and policy analysis: bill digests, version comparisons, policy options memos, fiscal evidence summaries, and district evidence checks.
• Member briefings: decision briefs, two-minute oral briefings, fact checks, position record digests, and briefing questions.
• Committee preparation: hearing memos, question sequences, markup preparation sheets, oversight evidence matrices, and follow-through trackers.
• Floor preparation: floor-day briefings, special-rule reading sheets, amendment status trackers, vote preparation cards, and post-vote updates.
• Stakeholder follow-through: policy meeting briefs, action records, evidence syntheses, district-to-policy handoffs, and factual FAQs.

## How to use the pack

• Choose one task. Start with an upcoming briefing, meeting, hearing, or staff handoff.
• Copy a prompt into ChatGPT Enterprise. Replace the bracketed placeholders with the bill number and Congress, issue, audience, deadline, or other requested context.
• Supply the source material. Paste approved text or attach the named documents where enabled. Include dates and bill versions. These basic chat prompts do not retrieve live records; a link alone is not source content.
• Review and refine. Check citations against the original documents, resolve missing inputs, and ask for changes to length, tone, or structure.
• Verify before use. Recheck time-sensitive details before briefing the Member or sharing the output.

## A useful first exercise

Try Bill digest with a public bill text. Ask for the 500-word digest, then compare its claims and section citations with the original. Once you are comfortable with that workflow, use Version comparison to examine two dated versions or Two-minute oral briefing to condense a staff memo.

## Keep judgment and review with people

Use only information approved for your office’s ChatGPT Enterprise workspace. Confirm bill identifiers, versions, vote questions, dates, quotations, and status. Treat every output as a draft for staff review. Staff and the Member retain decisions, positions, and commitments.
