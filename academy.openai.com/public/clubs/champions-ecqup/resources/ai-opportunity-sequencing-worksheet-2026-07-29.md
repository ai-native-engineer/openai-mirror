## 3. Compare your top AI opportunities

Capture the same six qualification signals across AI opportunities. Mark anything unconfirmed Unknown. Not every unknown must be tested immediately.

### 3a. Qualification signals

Rate each signal Strong, Mixed, Weak, or Unknown, and add a short evidence note for the rating.

* Strong means the signal clearly supports the opportunity

* Mixed means the evidence is uneven or conditional

* Weak means the signal adds little support

* Unknown means it has not been confirmed.

An AI opportunity does not need every signal to be Strong to qualify.

1. **Cost**: What spend or work could change? Consider external spend and internal preparation, review, or rework.

2. **Time**: Where could cycle time improve? Consider the full workflow, not only one faster task.

3. **Constraint**: What limits the work today? Identify the bottleneck, handoff, approval queue, quality issue, or capacity limit.

4. **Revenue**: What growth or revenue path could improve? If the connection is weak or not relevant, say so; the opportunity may still create value in other ways.

5. **Risk**: What risks could the opportunity reduce or introduce? Consider errors, quality failures, sensitive information, human review, and control needs; higher risk may require stronger safeguards or controls.

6. **Scale + importance**: How broadly and consequentially could this matter? Consider the people, teams, transactions, launches, or decisions affected and the importance of the work.

### 3b. Roadmap questions

Use the same four questions for every opportunity, then compare the answers.

1. Priority: Is the work and outcome specific and consequential enough for leadership to evaluate?

**Write down:** one recurring workflow; first users; intended outcome and why it matters now; the proposed accountable leader or ownership gap; workflow owner(s); the criteria for the next decision.

2. Value: Would changing this workflow address a real business constraint?

**Write down:** the current constraint; the baseline you know or need; the measurable outcome that would justify investment; and the quality, human-review, or control conditions that must hold.

3. Readiness: Can the organization support the first scope safely and reliably?

**Write down:** the required and the current Low / Medium / High level for:

* **Safeguards** (security + privacy)

* **Controls** (accountability, decision rights, approvals, human review, monitoring, and escalation),

* **Capacity** (people, time, skills, technical support, and operating support).

If the current state is not confirmed, mark it Unknown. Note the gap and who can confirm or close it.

4. Evidence: Which condition—Safeguards, Controls, or Capacity—is least proven, how broad and consequential is the uncertainty, and can it be resolved safely?

**Write down:** the unknown, its breadth, consequence, and resolvability, then size it as:

* **Bounded**: Narrow or narrowable, with contained downside and safely answerable now.

* **Addressable**: Material or consequential, with one closeable prerequisite that creates a credible path.

* **Foundational**: Broad or severe, with no safe path using current safeguards, controls, or capacity.

A known gap is a roadmap dependency. A factual unknown should be resolved through confirmation, approval, or an operating commitment.

Only use a bounded test for an empirical unknown if leadership approves prioritizing the AI opportunity. Use smallest credible confirmation or test, the evidence threshold, and how either result would change the recommendation.

## AI opportunity roadmap recommendation template

```
Based on [current evidence], we recommend [Now AI opportunity] for the next 90 days because [priority, value, and readiness].

﻿

﻿

We recommend [Next AI opportunity] for Next until [advance condition], and [Later AI opportunity] for Later until [review trigger].

﻿

Leadership must decide whether to approve [priority, owner, investment, and resources]. If approved, [Transformation Leader] will lead Design, Develop, and Operationalize with [Workflow Owner and other contributors], testing [least-proven condition] through [proof point].

﻿

﻿

At Day 90, use [evidence] to recommend whether leadership should advance, reshape, defer, or stop the Now AI opportunity and confirm or change the roadmap.
```

## 6. Turn the recommendation into a 90-Day Foundation Plan

You have already captured the inputs for the 90-Day Foundation Plan. Use **Build a Focused 90-Day Foundation Plan** asset in the **90-Day AI Transformation Starter Kit**, or paste the prompt below into ChatGPT with your completed worksheet, to generate a first-draft AI opportunity roadmap recommendation, stakeholder and ownership map, AI opportunity portfolio tracker, and 90-Day Foundation Plan.

#### Quick-start prompt

```
Using my completed AI Opportunity Sequencing Worksheet, generate four connected outputs: a credible AI opportunity roadmap recommendation, a stakeholder and ownership map, an AI opportunity portfolio tracker covering up to three AI opportunities, and a focused 90-Day Foundation Plan for the Now AI opportunity.

﻿

Reuse my business priority, up to three AI opportunities, six qualification signals, four roadmap questions, Now/Next/Later roadmap positions, intended outcome, accountable Transformation Leader, contributing Workflow Owner or workflow-specific ownership gap, least-proven condition, proof point, selected adoption and responsible-scale conditions, contributors, and blockers. Start with a leadership-ready AI opportunity roadmap recommendation explaining why the Now AI opportunity comes first, what must be true before the Next and Later AI opportunities advance, and which investment decision leadership must make. Keep the roadmap recommendation conditional until leadership confirms approval; do not invent an approved owner, investment, or resources.

﻿

Build the stakeholder and ownership map, AI opportunity portfolio tracker, and 90-Day Foundation Plan around four plan foundations: AI roadmap; organization-wide adoption; responsible scale; and business value. For each foundation, show what my worksheet establishes, a specific proposed action, the accountable Transformation Leader, contributing role, dependencies or safeguards, evidence or a decision checkpoint, and any missing item marked [TO CONFIRM].

﻿

After leadership approves the Now AI opportunity, show a phase-level 90-day plan. Design: confirm the bounded workflow, first users, baseline, accountable roles, required operating conditions, and learning goal. Develop: define the smallest useful delivery approach and the approved confirmation or bounded validation needed for the greatest unknown. Operationalize: plan first-user introduction, manager reinforcement, enablement and support, evidence tracking, and the Day-90 review. Do not invent detailed requirements, integrations, test cases, or rollout commitments unless they are present in the inputs or confirmed by the relevant owner. Phases may overlap.

﻿

Distinguish worksheet facts from proposed actions. Keep all three AI opportunities visible in the AI opportunity portfolio tracker; do not treat Next or Later as phases of Now or approved parallel work. Provide a concise [TO CONFIRM] list. Pre-draft the five-point opportunity summary for Now: Priority AI opportunity; Intended outcome; Accountable owner; Evidence needed; and 90-day action. Mark an unconfirmed owner [TO CONFIRM]. The accountable owner is the Transformation Leader driving the approved Now AI opportunity.

﻿

Review the generated outputs with the Executive Sponsor, contributing workflow owners, and relevant delivery, governance, and measurement partners. Confirm ownership, permissions, approvals, baselines, safeguards, escalation paths, and resources before treating the plan as approved.
```
