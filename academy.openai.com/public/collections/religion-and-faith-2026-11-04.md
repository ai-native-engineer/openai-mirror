### Greg Hamblin: Building a search engine for questions of faith

Greg Hamblin wanted to explore questions about his faith without searching the same websites one by one. With help from ChatGPT and Codex, he built LatterDaySearch, a website that searches sources he selected. Readers can ask questions in everyday language and find relevant articles—even when the authors use different words. The results link to original sources and highlight useful passages, helping Hamblin rediscover discussions and share them with others.](/public/blogs/greg-hamblin-building-a-search-engine-for-questions-of-faith-2026-10-08)[Blog

### Building an AI coach for deeper meditation

Meditation can leave people unsure whether they are making progress or what to try next. Stephen Zerfas, co-founder and CEO of Jhourney, is developing an AI tutor grounded in the company’s meditation curriculum and exploring coaching that responds as people describe their practice aloud. His goal is to extend the personalized guidance offered on retreats to people who cannot spend a week with an expert teacher.

### A software engineer builds a Muslim prayer app with ChatGPT and Codex

A near-fatal car crash led Kamran Mohammed and his wife to reconnect with Islam. Frustrated by worship apps crowded with ads or difficult for beginners, the Austin software engineer used ChatGPT and Codex to build My Amaal. The free, ad-free app offers illustrated prayer instructions and lets users track their prayers without sending their worship history to a company server.

### A rabbi uses ChatGPT to navigate centuries of Jewish thought

Rabbi Andrew Bloom uses a customized ChatGPT guide to find Jewish commentaries, then checks the original texts before deepening his research. His exploration of faith and technology also inspired The Ten Commandments of Ethical AI: From Mount Sinai to Silicon Valley, a book proposing standards for AI grounded in human dignity and accountability.
