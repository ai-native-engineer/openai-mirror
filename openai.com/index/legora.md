## Building for a profession where time—and trust—are scarce

Founded in Stockholm in 2023, [Legora⁠(opens in a new window)](https://www.legora.com/) is building what CEO and co-founder Max Junestrand calls “the agentic operating system for legal work”: bringing AI into the workflows lawyers rely on every day.

The company started with the insight that legal teams are unusually time crunched, meaning some valuable work doesn’t get done. Think of the junior lawyer told to review an impossible number of contracts overnight and only able to focus on a fraction.

Legora can provide those services “on tap”, which is why Junestrand’s ambition is that every piece of legal work in the world should run on his platform. OpenAI has been part of that journey from the beginning: the release of GPT‑3.5 was the moment many of the legal use cases the founding team had been experimenting with became possible. Today, OpenAI is a key model provider for Legora, powering core workflows across its legal AI platform, from agentic legal work to large-scale document review. Legora builds on new OpenAI model capabilities as they become available, translating advances in intelligence into what lawyers can accomplish with its product.

> “***The world needs more legal services and legal intelligence. With Legora, we get that on tap.***”

—Max Junestrand, CEO and co-founder, Legora

## An AI-native company building an AI-native product

Legora’s use of AI goes beyond the product it sells, and has expanded its use of ChatGPT Work, Codex and dots across its own teams. OpenAI’s technology is becoming part of both the product Legora builds for its customers as well as shaping how the company itself operates. “It’s incredibly important to me that we stay as AI-native as possible,” confirms Junestrand.

For Jacob Lauritzen, CTO at Legora, coding agents have changed how quickly the engineering team can develop new ideas. Instead of researching one possible solution and committing engineering time before knowing whether it’s the right path, teams can prototype many approaches at once. “We can explore **20 solutions at the same time** and, within days, pick the one we know will be the best.”

The same approach changes how Legora responds when something goes wrong. Previously, diagnosing a product issue could mean several engineers investigating different possible causes over a number of hours. Coding agents can now search millions of lines of logs and pursue many possible explanations simultaneously, sometimes taking Legora from bug report to working fix in **around five minutes**.

Customers, of course, don’t see the engineering efficiency itself. But they do experience the results: problems can be resolved faster, feedback can reach product teams sooner, and new ideas can be tested with customers earlier in the development cycle.

> “***We can go to customers much more quickly with feedback. It enables us to build a better product faster.***”

—Jacob Lauritzen, CTO, Legora

## Giving Legora’s own lawyers room to do higher-value work

Legora’s own legal team experiences the same transformation its product is designed to create for customers. General Counsel Emma Kadri Bergström points to the example of NDAs. Her team analysed the agreements they had historically signed and built a playbook that allows Legora to take a first pass at incoming NDAs, highlighting terms that fall outside acceptable parameters.

Before the workflow was introduced, Kadri Bergström estimates that reviewing these “boring documents” consumed around **20% of the legal team’s capacity**. Today, it’s **under 1%**.

That reclaimed time matters because the company itself is scaling rapidly. In the past year, Kadri Bergström says the business has expanded from **three offices to 18**, completed **three** **financing rounds** and made **five acquisitions**. The legal team has had to support that growth while remaining closely connected to the business.

AI has also allowed the team to take on work that might previously have been outsourced or narrowed because of limited capacity. In M&A diligence, for example, agents can begin reviewing contracts and surfacing information across the deal set, with lawyers reviewing and interpreting the output.

> ***“AI doesn’t just make you faster. It also increases quality.”***

—Emma Kadri Bergström, General Counsel, Legora

## From faster review to broader, higher-quality analysis

For Legora’s customers, the opportunity isn’t simply to complete the same work faster. AI has also increased the scope of work that can be taken on.

Alex Fortescue-Webb, Global Head of Legal Engineering, points to due diligence as an example. In a traditional review, time and cost constraints can force teams to concentrate deeply on only the most valuable contracts. Legora’s Tabular Review workflow can analyse thousands of contracts across multiple questions in minutes, allowing lawyers to apply detailed scrutiny much more broadly.

That can alter the quality of the final work. Risks that might previously have sat in contracts outside the manually reviewed subset can now be surfaced for a lawyer to assess.

The results are already measurable. Junestrand cites one Fortune 100 customer that has:

* reduced contract turnaround time by **90%**
* saved **more than $20 million** on external litigation and diligence
* returned **9.5 hours per lawyer each week** for higher-value work

> “***AI has fundamentally changed both the speed at which you can do existing tasks and the breadth of analysis you can apply.***”

—Alex Fortescue-Webb, Global Head of Legal Engineering, Legora

## Leadership lessons

* **Lead by using.** When more than 75% of leaders actively use Legora, median usage across the organisation is 3× higher.
* **Start with the problem.** Focus on what people need to accomplish, then determine where AI can improve the workflow.
* **Build a network of champions.** Internal advocates help turn experimentation into sustained adoption.
* **Be ready to rebuild.** As models improve, yesterday’s product assumptions can quickly become outdated.
* **Design for trust.** Build evaluation, testing and transparency into the experience from the start.

## What’s next

For Legora, the next frontier isn’t just making today’s legal workflows faster. It’s expanding the kinds of work AI can take on, while making collaboration between lawyers and AI increasingly natural.

Jacob Lauritzen sees two advances happening in parallel. First, agents will be able to tackle longer-running tasks that require far greater context and autonomy. He imagines due diligence spanning millions of pages running overnight, with the analysis ready for a lawyer to review when they arrive the next morning.

At the same time, increasingly capable models are becoming faster. That opens up a different kind of experience: real-time collaboration where a lawyer and AI can work back and forth on a contract, analysis or piece of advice rather than treating AI as a tool that receives a task and returns an answer.

> ***“When you blend human and AI, that’s when the output comes out best.”***

—Jacob Lauritzen, CTO, Legora

That moving frontier means Legora’s roadmap can’t be static. “Model improvements force me to reconsider the roadmap on a biweekly basis,” says Lauritzen. Engineers, product managers, designers and sales teams all frequently ask him what’s the new thing that’s become possible for customers.

For Bergström, it’s about changing the perception of legal teams. “Every legal department is worried about becoming a bottleneck. That’s when the business stops coming to you,” she says. AI is transforming legal teams from bottleneck to business partner, from the people you avoid to the people you want in the room alongside you.

If Legora succeeds, the outcome won’t simply be lawyers completing today’s workload more efficiently. It will be legal teams able to take on work that capacity once made impossible, with more time for judgement, strategy and the high-value decisions that still require a human.

As Junestrand puts it: “It’s the best time in history to be a lawyer.”
