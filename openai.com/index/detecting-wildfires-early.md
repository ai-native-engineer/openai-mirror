## A fire close to home

After seeing the devastation of the 2018 Camp Fire in fifth grade, Ryan Honary thought there had to be a better way. He turned that thought into a science project, using a few wires, a heat detector, and a hair dryer.

Five years later, that science project became SensoRy AI—a wildfire detection system that identifies fires at ignition and immediately alerts firefighters and officials. ChatGPT translates the complex sensor data into clear, actionable information responders can quickly and easily understand.

## Detecting the first signs

Wildfires often begin in remote terrain where canyons, vegetation, and limited visibility make early detection difficult. Cameras, satellites, and human spotters may miss the earliest signs of a fire.

SensoRy AI’s solar-powered sensors detect heat, smoke, flame, and plume activity at ignition. That information is relayed through a mesh network, while ChatGPT translates the live sensor data into clear alerts responders can understand the moment a potential fire is detected.

## Turning alerts into answers

Detecting a fire is only the first step. The real challenge is turning complex sensor data into answers people can act on. Ryan’s sensors analyze thermal images, smoke, GPS coordinates, wind conditions, and potential fire spread. ChatGPT helps translate that information into clear, immediate guidance for firefighters and residents.

Using ChatGPT, Ryan built a walkie-talkie feature that lets firefighters directly ask questions like: Is this a fire? Where is it located? What is the best evacuation route? ChatGPT turns live sensor data into answers in seconds.

## From science fair to sensor network

What started as a fifth-grade science project is now a working wildfire detection system in Southern California. SensoRy AI has already detected fires in the field, is deployed across three cities, and is expanding toward more than 100 sensors across the state.

As Ryan prepares to leave home for college, he continues to work with firefighters, researchers, and land managers to improve the network with every deployment. His goal is to help firefighters respond sooner, give residents clearer information faster, and make communities safer from wildfires.
