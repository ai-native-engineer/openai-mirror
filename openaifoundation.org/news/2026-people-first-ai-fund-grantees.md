# Supporting Communities: Meet the 2026 People-First AI Fund Grantees

Our second People-First AI Fund is deploying $50 million to 163 nonprofits exploring how AI can help communities across the United States access essential services, strengthen creative and cultural institutions, and support local journalism.

By OpenAI Foundation

Community Support Services

Today the OpenAI Foundation is announcing the [selection of 163 organizations⁠](#2026-people-first-ai-fund-grantees) to receive $50 million in total through the 2026 People-First AI Fund. The organizations are based in 35 U.S. states and Washington, D.C., and serve rural and urban communities across the country. This second cohort builds on the [initial $50 million People-First AI Fund⁠](https://openai.com/index/people-first-ai-fund/) the Foundation launched in 2025.

Through unrestricted grants, we’re giving these trusted institutions the resources and flexibility to explore how AI can strengthen their work—and how the people and communities they serve can engage with and shape its use. The Fund supports nonprofits at every stage of AI learning and experimentation, from those just beginning to ask what the technology could make possible to those piloting tools they co-created with communities. Our goal is to support their leadership as they work to understand this technology, its impacts, and the use cases that best serve their missions.

Local nonprofits are often where people turn first: to find help with housing or disability benefits, to experience artistic joy, or to understand a decision that affects their neighborhood. Building on lessons from the 2025 Fund and feedback from communities, this year’s Fund focuses on three priorities: community support services, community arts and cultural organizations, and community journalism and media.

By design, the Fund is product-agnostic: grantees have full autonomy to choose—or not—any tool or platform that they believe best serves their work and communities. Applicants described a range of AI products they are already using or are interested in learning more about, as well as the open questions they still have and want to explore about use of AI in their communities.

Most of this year’s grantees are new partners to the Fund, alongside a small number of returning grantees. We highlight some of the grantees below, and the full list can be found in alphabetical order [here⁠](#2026-people-first-ai-fund-grantees).

## Community Support Services

![Visitors explore musical instruments and cultural objects displayed on a table with a presenter.](/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimages.ctfassets.net%2Fotoa9rt8o2ha%2F6lsplekZABsfwUHs1PV7Aw%2Fc4f13feb8ada9f16a9d447e6616b84b8%2FNigerian_Center_1.jpg&w=3840&q=75)

Credit: Nigerian Center

Community-based nonprofits are trusted navigators, helping people access legal aid, housing, food, disability services, and other essential benefits. They help people understand their options and work through systems that can be difficult to navigate alone, especially where language barriers, complicated processes, or limited local services make getting help harder.

Across the portfolio, many organizations are exploring AI to improve administration and operations, with the goal of giving staff more time with the people they serve. Community-based nonprofits also see opportunities to translate and synthesize information, understand and explain complicated benefits policies, and simplify applications so people can get clear, relevant guidance when they need it.

> **Meet a few of our grantees**
>
> [**Greater Bakersfield Legal Assistance**⁠](https://gbla.org/) **(CA)** provides free civil legal services to Kern County residents facing poverty, housing instability, and disability, and is piloting AI tools to support intake and multilingual resources.
>
> [**JustFix**⁠](https://www.justfix.org/en/) **(NY)**is exploring AI tools to help renters understand rent records and navigate eviction, with renters, organizers, and legal aid partners shaping the work.
>
> [**Rural Opportunity Institute**⁠](https://www.ruralopportunity.org/)**(NC)** helps rural schools access public funding for student mental health services. Using AI to support back-office operations, they helped partner schools double Medicaid revenue and triple the number of students receiving mental health services.

## Community Arts and Cultural Organizations

![Dancers in red, orange, and yellow costumes leap together onstage with their arms raised.](/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimages.ctfassets.net%2Fotoa9rt8o2ha%2F25yFPv3PIJoWulUEzaZGOU%2Fe88e6de0e8467746803dc4cf230c88ba%2FParsons_Dance_2.jpeg.jpg&w=3840&q=75)

Credit: Parsons Dance (Paula Lobo)

Arts and cultural organizations are a front door to creative experiences, offering places to hear music, make art, explore local history, and connect with neighbors. They give people opportunities to participate in cultural life and help communities preserve and share their stories.

Many applicants expressed interest in exploring or using AI to strengthen and sustain participation in their work—such as supporting donor outreach, understanding their audiences, or helping with back-office administration, with the hope of giving small teams more time to support artists and community programming. Others are exploring how AI can support creative work through new forms of performance, storytelling, and participation, with artists and communities shaping how the tools are used.

> **Meet a few of our grantees**
>
> [**Nashville Jazz Workshop**⁠](https://www.nashvillejazz.org/) **(TN)** plans to make its concert archive searchable and explore an AI listening companion that helps newcomers discover jazz.
>
> [**August Wilson African American Cultural Center**⁠](https://awaacc.org/) **(PA)** plans to use AI to ease administrative work and help staff spend more time supporting artists and community programming.
>
> [**Library Foundation of Los Angeles**⁠](https://lfla.org/) **(CA)** supports the [Los Angeles Public Library⁠](https://www.lapl.org/) and its 73 locations in expanding equitable learning opportunities and providing the community with free access to emerging AI technologies.

## Community Journalism and Media

![A sticker reading “The LA Local” on a surface covered in layered stickers, posters, and graffiti.](/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fimages.ctfassets.net%2Fotoa9rt8o2ha%2F6gHeJYXoi6XlmuHiuaN5Dl%2F8efbc23c71efe6336e01375d1b115929%2FThe_LA_Local_1.jpg&w=3840&q=75)

Credit: Leroy Hamilton for The LA Local

Local newsrooms are exploring and using AI to support new business models, strengthen reporting, and make reliable information easier to find and use. A common theme across applicants and selected grantees was exploring these tools to increase civic participation and engagement, including by translating reporting to make it accessible to more audiences, opening up public records, and helping residents understand decisions affecting their lives.

The Fund has chosen to support that work with direct grants to a wide range of local newsrooms and media organizations, as well as grants that help expand the Fund’s support to an even broader set of outlets. This includes organizations like the [American Journalism Project⁠](https://theajp.org/), which is strengthening the nonprofit local news field by investing capital and strategic support in a network of community-rooted newsrooms, and the [Colorado News Collaborative⁠](https://colabnews.co/), which connects 200 local news organizations and is coordinating an AI learning network across the state.

> **Meet a few of our grantees**
>
> [**Honolulu Civil Beat**⁠](https://www.civilbeat.org/) **(HI)** uses AI to make public records about the state Legislature easier to search. The newsroom also offers free training to help residents use that information to participate in government.
>
> [**California Black Media**⁠](https://cablackmedia.org/) **(CA)** connects and strengthens a network of 30 Black-owned media outlets, helping publishers explore AI for newsroom operations and generate new sources of revenue to sustain their work.
>
> [**Free Press Indiana**⁠](https://www.freepressindiana.org/) **(IN)** supports two hyperlocal newsrooms focused on neighborhood and rural community news, housing, local government, and civic accountability, and is exploring AI for translation and accessibility.

## Our Process

The Fund’s open call received 1,669 applications from across the country. Daniel Zingale, who convened the [OpenAI Nonprofit Commission⁠](https://openai.com/index/nonprofit-commission-report/), worked with a set of external advisors to develop and implement the 2026 review process. Every application received was reviewed by an experienced external grantmaker, with selection emphasizing community trust, demonstrated impact, and a thoughtful approach to exploring AI.

Given the significant demand, we will be distributing the full $50 million to organizations who applied through the open call. We aim to disburse every grant in full before the end of 2026, putting resources immediately into organizations’ hands so they can move their urgent work forward.
